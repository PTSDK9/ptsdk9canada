Trained Capabilities

What a PTSD service dog actually does

These are not comfort animals. Every task below is trained to a specific symptom of PTSD and recognized under Canadian law as a medical accommodation.

Nightmare Interruption

The dog detects rapid breathing, thrashing, or distress sounds during sleep and wakes you with a nudge or paw before the nightmare cycle sets in.

Panic & Anxiety Interrupt

When the dog detects your physical signs of escalating anxiety — a trained response brings you back before a panic attack takes hold.

Deep Pressure Therapy

The dog applies body weight during dissociative episodes or flashbacks — grounding you to the present moment through physical sensation.

Room Search & Clear

On command, the dog enters a room ahead of you, moves through the space, and returns with a signal that the area is safe — reducing hypervigilance triggers.

Crowd Buffering

The dog positions behind or in front of you in public spaces — physically creating personal space so you can move through crowds without triggering threat responses.

Grounding During Flashbacks

The dog nudges, paws, or licks on cue to interrupt dissociation or flashback episodes — pulling attention back to the physical present.

Crisis Retrieval & Alerts

The dog retrieves a phone, medication, or alerts another person in the home during a crisis — giving you a path out of escalating moments.

Medication Reminders

Trained to alert at set times to prompt medication or routine check-ins — supporting consistency during low-functioning periods.

How It Works

The path from application to partnership

Every placement and training program is built around you — your history, your symptoms, your life. Here is what the process looks like from start to finish.

1
Weeks 1–2

Application & Intake

You submit your application below. Our intake coordinator reviews your background, trauma history, and needs — then reaches out by email to schedule a private consultation. No details are shared without your consent.

2
Weeks 3–4

Assessment & Task Planning

We work with you (and where possible, your mental health provider) to map your specific PTSD symptoms to a custom task list. This determines which training model your dog will follow and whether you are applying for a program dog or bringing your own.

3
Weeks 5–16

Training Program

Dogs undergo structured training aligned to your assessed needs. If you are owner-training your dog, you will work alongside a trainer throughout this period. Sessions are built around trauma-informed practice — we move at your pace.

4
Weeks 17–20

Placement & Team Training

You and your dog train as a team in real environments — public spaces, transit, home routines. The goal is that you trust the dog and the dog knows you before any graduation.

5
Week 20+

Graduation & Ongoing Support

Your team passes a Public Access Test in multiple locations. After graduation, we remain available for follow-up support, task refreshers, and any adjustments as your needs evolve.

Who Can Apply

Eligibility & what to expect

We serve survivors across Canada. Our program is built for civilians who have experienced violence, assault, sexual abuse, domestic trauma, and other forms of complex PTSD — not only veterans or first responders.

You may be eligible if you…

  • Have a PTSD or CPTSD diagnosis from a licensed mental health provider
  • Are a survivor of domestic violence, sexual assault, forcible confinement, or prolonged abuse
  • Experience hypervigilance, nightmares, panic attacks, dissociation, or flashbacks
  • Are a Canadian resident (all provinces and territories)
  • Have the ability to provide a stable home environment for a dog
  • Are currently under the care of a mental health professional or willing to be
  • Are open to the full training program (approx. 20 weeks)

What we ask of you

  • Honesty in your application — the more we understand, the better we can match you
  • A commitment to the training process, even on difficult days
  • Willingness to involve your mental health provider where possible
  • A home assessment (conducted by email and video call)
  • For own-dog applicants: your dog must pass an initial temperament assessment
  • Participation in ongoing check-ins after placement

Your rights under Canadian law. In Canada, psychiatric service dogs are protected under the Canadian Human Rights Act and the Accessible Canada Act. A qualified service dog team has the right to access any public space, housing, and transportation where the public is allowed. In Ontario, only a doctor's letter is currently required. We can advise you on documentation for your specific province upon application.
